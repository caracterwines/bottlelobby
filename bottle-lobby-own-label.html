Beyond A Pretty Label

Design is the last step, not the first.

A label isn't just an artistic exercise for us — it's a research process. Before any design work begins, we analyze the market and define exactly who the bottle needs to speak to. Only then do we develop the design and the wine's name together, checked against the current cultural moment and tested with real buyers, not just assumed to work.

But the real work happens even earlier than that. We spend real time understanding how the wine is actually made and the philosophy behind the estate — the decisions, the values, the person behind the bottle — because that's what needs to end up on the label, not an afterthought bolted onto a finished wine. Our motto for this whole service is simple: a message in a bottle. Every wine carries a story; our job is making sure it actually reaches the person holding the bottle.

That's where the back label earns its place. We use it to tell the story properly — a poem written by the winemaker, the history of the land itself, or a family's own account of how the estate came to be. For a Lebanese wine, that might mean 1,100 years of winemaking tradition. For another producer, it might be four generations working the same hillside. Either way, the back label is what catches the customer once the bottle is already in their hands — proof that there's more inside than what's poured out.

From there, we make sure the bottle is market-ready and genuinely compelling — not just through design, but through play. One approach we use: giving each wine in a portfolio its own animal, chosen to match that wine's character, so every bottle from an estate has a personality a customer can recognize at a glance. This is what we mean by going beyond traditional labeling — considering not only what a label denotes, the grape, the region, the facts, but what it connotes, the feeling it creates before a single word is read.

A Closer Look

Every element earns its place.

A hand-drawn grape motif ties every own-label bottle back to the same visual family, regardless of varietal or vintage. The varietal and appellation are always named plainly — no invented estate, no borrowed prestige. And "one distributor, one region" isn't decoration: it's the actual commercial term written into every own-label agreement.

How It Works

From flagged wine
to protected exclusive.

Four steps turn one of your wines into a fully protected, exclusively distributed product — with no separate paperwork outside the platform.

01

Flag It Available

A winery marks a wine "Own-Label Available" in their Wine Portfolio — no new listing, just a toggle on a wine that already exists.

02

A Distributor Claims It

A matched distributor requests exclusive rights, locking in price, minimum volume, and branding terms for the contract period.

03

Delisted From Competition

The exact bottle is withdrawn from open competition — no other distributor on Bottle Lobby can offer that identical wine.

04

Sell at Stable Prices

Distributors and restaurants sell under locked pricing for the full term — no race to the bottom, no surprise increases.
